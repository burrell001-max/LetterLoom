Scrabble Strategy Guide: Score More Every Game

By Kent Williams · Updated November 14, 2026

Scrabble rewards vocabulary, but the players who win consistently also manage their racks and the board. These ideas apply equally to Words With Friends and other tile games.

1. Think about what you keep, not only what you score

The tiles you hold after a play are your leave. A play that scores 20 and leaves a balanced rack is often better than one that scores 24 and leaves you with four vowels. Aim for roughly three consonants to every two vowels.

  • Good tiles to keep: S, the blank, and flexible letters such as E, R, T, N, A, I.
  • Tiles to avoid duplicating: two or more of the same letter, especially V, W, I, U.
  • Q without U: play it or exchange it. See Q words with no U.

2. Fish for bingos

Using all seven tiles in one turn earns a 50-point bonus in Scrabble. Common seven- and eight-letter words come from rack leaves with endings such as -ING, -ER, -ED, -IES and -ENT. When your rack is strong, play off a few weak tiles and keep a high-probability leave. Use the solver's Anagrams tab to see whether your seven tiles make a word.

3. Respect premium squares

  • The first move covers the centre star, which doubles the word.
  • Triple word squares sit on the board edges. Do not open one for your opponent without a good reason.
  • A double-double (two double word squares in one play) is worth four times the base word.
  • Put heavy letters on letter squares that also form a second word.

4. Use hooks and parallel plays

A hook is a letter added to the front or back of an existing word, commonly S, D, R, or Y. Playing a word that hooks and forms a new word scores both. Parallel plays, where several tiles sit alongside tiles on the board, score all the two-letter words they form. Learn the full list of two-letter words.

5. Track the tiles

The set has 100 tiles, including two blanks. There is one each of J, K, Q, X and Z and twelve Es. Knowing what has been played tells you what your opponent is likely holding, and helps in the endgame.

6. Play the endgame carefully

When the bag is empty, going out first gives you the sum of your opponent's remaining tile values, and they lose that amount. Plan your last two or three turns so you can go out, and keep high-value tiles from being stuck on your rack.

7. Defence matters

Before you play, ask what you are opening. A word ending in a letter that takes an S hook next to a triple word square gives your opponent a big turn. Sometimes a smaller, closed play is better.

8. Know when to exchange

If your rack is poorly balanced and nothing scores acceptably, exchange tiles. Keeping a good leave and drawing fresh tiles beats forcing a weak play.

Practise

Study short words first, then common endings. Check your racks against the solver after a game to see what you missed. Remember that our word list is not identical to the official Scrabble dictionary, so check disputed words in yours.
