Two-Letter Words for Scrabble and Words With Friends

By Kent Williams · Updated November 14, 2026

Two-letter words are the most useful short words in any tile game. They let you play parallel to tiles already on the board, squeeze into tight spots, and score on two words at once.

Important: check your game's dictionary

Our list is ENABLE, an open dictionary. Current official North American Scrabble lists accept a few more two-letter words that are missing here, including QI, ZA and OK. Words With Friends uses its own dictionary too. If a word you want is not in the solver, confirm it in your game before assuming it is invalid.

Two-letter words with high-value letters

These are the plays to memorise first, because they put J, X and other heavy tiles onto the board with very little room:

  • JO (Scrabble 9 points): the only two-letter J word in this list.
  • AX, EX, OX (9 points each) and XI, XU (9 points each): an X on a premium square next to an existing vowel can score heavily because it forms two words.
  • KA (6 points): the way to unload a K in this list.

Vowel-only two-letter words

If your rack is full of vowels, these help you dump them: AA, AE, AI, OE.

Words with no vowels

These are valid and often forgotten: HM, MM, SH. They are lifesavers when you are holding only consonants.

How to use two-letter words to score

  1. Parallel plays. Lay a word alongside an existing word so every tile you place also forms a two-letter word with the tile next to it. You score every word formed.
  2. Hook plays. Add a single tile to the end or front of a word already on the board, then play a long word in the other direction.
  3. Premium squares. Put a high-value tile on a double or triple letter square so that it counts in two directions.
  4. Defence. Knowing which two-letter words exist helps you spot openings your opponent can use before you leave them.
