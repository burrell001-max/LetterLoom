How to use this word unscrambler

  1. Type the letters from your rack or puzzle. Use ? or * for blank tiles.
  2. Choose a game tab: Scrabble, Words With Friends, Wordfeud, Wordscapes, Word Cookies or Anagrams.
  3. Narrow the list with a word length chip, or with the Starts with, Ends with and Contains boxes.
  4. Tap any word to see its tile points and a short definition.

How the solver works

Your letters are checked against a 168,000-word list inside your browser, so nothing is sent to a server and results arrive in milliseconds. Each blank tile can stand in for any letter and scores zero points. Scrabble and Words With Friends modes rank words by tile value; Wordscapes shows words of three or more letters; Anagrams mode lists only words that use every letter.

The word list is ENABLE, an open dictionary. It is close to, but not identical to, the official Scrabble lists, so check disputed words against your game's own dictionary.

Tips for higher scores

Look for high-value letters (J, Q, X, Z) on premium squares, and keep a balanced rack of vowels and consonants. Short words that use a Q without a U, or that fit two-letter plays beside existing tiles, often outscore longer words. Using all seven tiles in Scrabble earns a 50-point bonus, so check the Anagrams tab when your rack looks promising.

Scrabble and Words With Friends strategy in brief

Balance your rack, keep flexible tiles such as S, E, R and T, and avoid holding duplicates or a Q without a U. Put heavy tiles (J, X, Z, Q) on letter premium squares where they form two words, and learn the two-letter words to play parallel to tiles on the board. Fish for seven-letter bingos when your rack allows: 50 bonus points in Scrabble, fewer in Words With Friends. Read the full Scrabble strategy guide or see how the two games differ.

About our word list

The solver uses ENABLE, a list of about 168,500 two- to fifteen-letter words, plus a small set of modern additions such as QI, ZA and OK. It is large and fast, but not identical to official Scrabble dictionaries, which have more recent words. The Word list menu also offers an Everyday words list and a UK spellings list, both built from free SCOWL word lists. Read how our word list compares to official ones.

Frequently asked questions

How do I unscramble letters into words?

Type your letters into the box at the top of the page. The solver checks them against a dictionary of about 168,000 words and lists every word you can make, longest and highest scoring first. Results update as you type.

How do blank tiles and wildcards work?

Type ? or * for each blank tile. A blank can stand in for any letter, so ?A? will find words like CAT, BAD and EAR. In Scrabble and Words With Friends a blank scores zero points, and the solver reflects that.

What is the difference between the Scrabble and Words With Friends modes?

The two games use different tile values. For example, Q is worth 10 in both, but H is 4 in Scrabble and 3 in Words With Friends, and U is 1 in Scrabble but 2 in Words With Friends. Pick your game and the point values next to each word change to match.

Can I find words that start or end with a certain letter?

Yes. Use the Starts with, Ends with and Contains boxes under the game tabs. You can also tick Use all letters to see only true anagrams, and tap a length chip to show only words of that size.

Which dictionary does the solver use?

By default it uses ENABLE, an open word list of about 168,000 words. The Word list menu also offers a smaller Everyday words list and a UK spellings list. None of these is an official Scrabble dictionary, so check any disputed word against your game's own dictionary before you play it.

Does it work for Wordfeud and Word Cookies?

Yes. Pick the Wordfeud tab for the official English Wordfeud tile values and a 40-point bonus note, or the Word Cookies tab for words of three or more letters. Both games use their own dictionaries, so a rare word may be accepted in one and not the other.

What is the Everyday words list?

It is a smaller list of common English words, built from the free SCOWL word lists and limited to words that are also in ENABLE. Use it for casual puzzles. Switch back to the full list for Scrabble-style play, where obscure words are legal.

What does the UK spellings list add?

It adds British spellings such as colour, centre and realise to the full ENABLE list, using free SCOWL word lists. It helps with UK-style puzzles. It is not the official Collins list, so check disputed words in your own game.

Is my data sent to a server?

No. Solving happens entirely in your browser, so the letters you type are not uploaded. Tapping a word may look up its definition from a free third-party dictionary service. See the Privacy Policy for details.

What is Wordscapes mode?

Wordscapes puzzles accept words of three or more letters, so this mode hides two-letter words and sorts by length. Anagrams mode lists only words that use every one of your letters.
