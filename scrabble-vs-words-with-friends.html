Scrabble vs Words With Friends: Key Differences

By Kent Williams · Updated November 14, 2026

The two games look alike and share most of their rules, but a few differences change which words are best. Playing Words With Friends with Scrabble habits can cost you points.

In practice, B, C, M and P (4 instead of 3), G, L, N, U (one point more each), V and J are worth more in Words With Friends, while H and Y are worth one point less. Use the game tabs in the solver to see the right points for each.

Other differences

  • Board layout. The premium squares are arranged differently, so triple word squares are in other positions than in Scrabble. Learn the layout of the game you play.
  • Bingo bonus. Scrabble pays 50 points for using all seven tiles. Words With Friends pays a smaller bonus, usually reported as 35 points, so long plays are slightly less decisive.
  • Invalid words. Scrabble players can challenge a word, and a wrong challenge or play costs a turn or points. Words With Friends checks the dictionary automatically and rejects invalid plays, so you can test words freely.
  • Dictionary. Each game uses its own word list, so a word can be valid in one and not in the other.
  • Format. Scrabble is usually played face to face or on a single board, while Words With Friends is asynchronous, with games lasting days.

The exact details of each game can change with updates, so check the in-game rules for the current bonuses and dictionary.

Strategy changes

Because you cannot be penalised for trying an invalid word, test unusual plays in Words With Friends. Because the bingo bonus is smaller, a solid 40-point play can beat fishing for seven-letter words. Keep the tips from the Scrabble strategy guide on rack balance and hooks.
